Deep, restorative sleep is front-loaded — most of it happens in the first half of the night. REM sleep (dream sleep, emotional processing) dominates the second half. Cortisol isn't just a stress hormone. It's the thing standing between you and deep sleep — and most people have no idea it's the reason they can't wind down.

Reason 01

Cortisol and melatonin are in direct opposition

These two hormones follow opposing curves across the day. Melatonin rises as cortisol falls in the evening, signaling to your brain that it's time to sleep. When cortisol doesn't fall on schedule — because stress kept it elevated — melatonin can't rise properly. Reason 03

Chronic stress flattens your cortisol curve

Healthy cortisol follows a clear curve: high in the morning, declining through the day, low at night. Chronic stress flattens this curve — cortisol stays moderately elevated around the clock rather than following its natural rhythm. The result is a nervous system that's never fully alert and never fully calm. You feel foggy during the day and wired at night — not because cortisol is spiking, but because it never fully drops.
